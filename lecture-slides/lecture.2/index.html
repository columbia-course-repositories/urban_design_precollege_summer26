Observation, Vantage Points, and Urban Representation

  • Observation: Learning to see and interpret the built environment through careful attention to urban space.
  • Vantage Points: Understanding how location, orientation, and perspective shape what we notice and how we experience cities.
  • Urban Representation: Using maps, sketches, photographs, and diagrams to communicate and analyze urban conditions.

Four Ways to Represent a Place

  • Representation:
    • Map
    • Photograph
    • Sketch
    • Diagram

 

  • Key idea: Each representation reveals some things and hides others.

What Maps Do Well

  • Maps show:
    • Location
    • Distance
    • Spatial relationships
    • Boundaries
  • Maps often miss:
    • Atmosphere
    • Experience
    • Texture

What Photographs Do Well

  • Photographs show:
    • Appearance
    • Detail
    • Surface conditions
    • A moment in time
  • Photographs often miss:
    • Interpretation
    • Emphasis
    • Spatial hierarchy

What Sketches Do Well

  • Sketches show:
    • Spatial relationships
    • Proportion and massing
    • Visual hierarchy
    • What the observer considers important
  • Sketches often miss:
    • Precise detail
    • Surface texture
    • Exact dimensions

What Diagrams Do Well

  • Diagrams show:
    • Patterns
    • Relationships
    • Systems
    • Analytical ideas
  • Diagrams often miss:
    • Physical appearance
    • Atmosphere
    • Material qualities

Urban Sketching Is Not Art

  • Urban sketching in this course is about:

    • Observing
    • Representing
    • Analyzing

  • It is not about making beautiful drawings.

  • Key Concept: The sketch is evidence.

What Urban Designers Actually Draw

  • Focus on:
    • Building mass, street edge and sidewalks
    • Open space, trees
    • Movement, boundaries and landmarks
  • Avoid obsessing over:
    • Every window, brick, person and decorative details
