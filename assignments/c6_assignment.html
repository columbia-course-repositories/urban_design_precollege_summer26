🎯 Characterizing Place

Overview

Cities, suburbs, exurbs, and rural landscapes are not always easy to distinguish. Population density, development patterns, transportation systems, and historical growth all influence how places function and how they are experienced.

In this assignment, we will use GIS data, remote sensing, and mapping tools to investigate a location and develop an evidence-based characterization of that place.

Our goal is not to find a single correct answer. Our goal is to gather evidence and construct a reasoned argument using maps, imagery, and spatial data.

Important

Important: The goal of this assignment is not to determine a single correct classification. Instead, use multiple forms of evidence to develop and defend an interpretation of your study area.

🎓 Learning Objectives

By completing this assignment, students will:

  • Interpret population density data.
  • Investigate urban growth and expansion.
  • Compare urban, suburban, exurban, and rural landscapes.
  • Explore metropolitan regions and settlement patterns.
  • Use GIS to support evidence-based conclusions.
  • Synthesize multiple forms of spatial evidence into a coherent interpretation.

Before beginning your own investigation, we will explore a common study area together using several GIS datasets.

The purpose of this exercise is to understand what each dataset represents, how it is visualized, and how it can be used to understand urban growth and change.

🔍 Part 2 — Independent Investigation

Choose a Study Area

Select a location that is meaningful to you.

Examples include:

  • Your hometown
  • Your current residence
  • A city you have visited
  • A place of personal interest

The study area may be located in the United States or internationally.

Important

Important: If you choose an international location, consult with instructor on best location for World Pop population density data.

📍 Question 1 — Where Is This Place?

Create a map showing the location of your study area.

Consider:

  • Country
  • State or Province
  • Region
  • Relationship to nearby cities

Prompt

How would you describe the location of your study area to someone unfamiliar with it?

👥 Question 2 — How Many People Appear to Live Here?

Use WorldPop population density data.

Prompt

What does the population density suggest about this place? Is population concentrated or dispersed?

🌃 Question 3 — Has This Place Grown?

Use available historical evidence.

Possible sources:

  • Harmonized Global Night Time Lights
  • Historical imagery
  • Atlas of Urban Expansion
  • Other historical spatial evidence

Prompt

What evidence suggests that this place has expanded, intensified, or changed over time?

🏘️ Question 4 — What Does This Place Look Like?

Use:

  • OpenStreetMap
  • Satellite imagery
  • Aerial imagery

Consider:

  • Streets
  • Buildings
  • Open space
  • Agricultural land
  • Transportation infrastructure
  • Land use patterns

Prompt

What patterns of development are visible in your study area?

🔗 Question 5 — How Is This Place Connected?

United States Locations

Use:

  • Core Based Statistical Areas (CBSA)
  • Urban Areas

International Locations

Use:

  • Regional context
  • Nearby cities
  • Atlas of Urban Expansion (where available)

Prompt

How is this place connected to nearby settlements or metropolitan regions?

🧭 Question 6 — How Would You Characterize This Place?

Based on all evidence collected, characterize your study area.

Possible categories include:

  • Urban Core
  • Urban Neighborhood
  • Suburban
  • Exurban
  • Rural
  • Mixed
  • Transitional

Prompt

Based on population density, evidence of growth, urban form, and regional context, how would you characterize this place? What evidence most strongly supports your conclusion?

💭 Reflection

Prompt

Did any of the datasets challenge your initial assumptions about this place? Why or why not?

📄 Deliverable

Create a single landscape-oriented PDF exported from QGIS Layout Manager.

Important

Important: Your final PDF should present evidence and interpretation. Maps alone are not sufficient. Each panel should include brief written observations explaining what the data suggests about your study area.

The PDF can and should include the following components:

Panel 1 — Study Area

  • Location map
  • Regional context
  • Brief description

Panel 2 — Population Density

  • WorldPop population density visualization
  • Short interpretation

Panel 3 — Urban Growth

  • Night Lights and/or historical evidence
  • Short interpretation

Panel 4 — Ground Truth

  • OpenStreetMap
  • Satellite or aerial imagery
  • Short interpretation

Panel 5 — Characterization Statement

Write approximately 150–250 words addressing:

  • What is this place?
  • Why?
  • What evidence supports your conclusion?

📚 Reading for Class 8

Before Class 8, complete the following reading:

Required Reading

  • Anthony Williams and Ryan Avent, Demand for Density: The Functions of the City in the 21st Century (Brookings Institution)

    • Link to Reading

Reading Questions

As you read, consider the following questions:

  1. Why do cities continue to attract people and investment?
  2. What advantages does density provide?
  3. How does density support economic, social, and cultural activity?
  4. Why might dense urban environments remain important in an increasingly digital world?
  5. How do the authors explain the continued growth of cities in the twenty-first century?
  6. How does the reading relate to the patterns of urban growth and settlement that you investigated in this assignment?
  7. How might cities adapt and transform as technologies, industries, and populations change over time?
Important

Important: Be prepared to discuss the reading during the opening discussion of Class 8. Consider how the ideas presented in the article relate to urban growth, population density, metropolitan regions, and future urban development.
