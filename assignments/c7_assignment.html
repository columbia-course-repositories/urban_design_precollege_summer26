🏙️ From Urban Growth to Adaptive Reuse

In Class 7, we examined why cities grow, why density matters, and how outward expansion can create environmental and economic costs through land consumption, infrastructure extension, and transportation dependence.

In Class 8, we turn to a different question:

How can cities accommodate change by reusing, repairing, remediating, and reimagining what already exists?

Adaptive reuse is not limited to historic buildings. It can involve industrial land, rail corridors, waterfronts, utility structures, former manufacturing districts, contaminated sites, and other inherited urban conditions. These places may appear obsolete or underused, but they often contain physical, environmental, infrastructural, historical, and social resources that can shape future urban development.

This assignment asks you to investigate one NYC Place-Based Community Brownfield Planning Area using planning documents, maps, images, and community-planning materials available through the NYC Environmental Review and Permitting Information Center (EPIC).

🎯 Assignment Question

How does a Place-Based Community Brownfield Planning Area transform an inherited industrial, environmental, or infrastructural condition into a future urban possibility?

Your group will examine an existing planning area and identify:

  • What exists there now.
  • What environmental, industrial, infrastructural, or land-use issue matters.
  • What future transformation is proposed or being considered.
  • What public benefit could result.
  • What concern, tradeoff, or unresolved question remains.

👥 Group Work

  • Work in your assigned group.
  • Each group will select or receive one NYC EPIC Place-Based Community Brownfield Planning Area.
  • Your group will research, organize, and present one concise Brownfield Transformation Profile.
  • This is an in-class research and presentation assignment.

🧭 Part 1: Select and Investigate a Planning Area

Review the EPIC Place-Based Community Brownfield Planning Areas and select one planning area for group investigation.

As you research, identify the following:

📍 Location and Context

  • What borough and neighborhood is the planning area located in?
  • What is the geographic extent of the planning area?
  • What streets, waterfronts, rail corridors, parks, industrial districts, or other urban features help define the area?

🏗️ Existing Condition

  • What currently exists in the planning area?
  • What land uses, buildings, infrastructure, open spaces, waterfront conditions, or industrial remnants are present?
  • What physical, environmental, or spatial conditions appear important?

🧪 Environmental, Industrial, or Infrastructural Issue

  • Is there a brownfield, contamination, industrial legacy, infrastructure barrier, waterfront condition, vacancy issue, or other constraint?
  • How does this issue affect the planning area?
  • What evidence from the planning materials helps explain the condition?

🔄 Future Transformation

  • What reuse, remediation, redevelopment, public-space, housing, mobility, environmental, or community strategy is proposed or being considered?
  • What existing assets might be retained, repaired, revealed, transformed, or removed?
  • How does the planning area imagine a different future for the site or neighborhood?

🌱 Public Benefit

  • What public benefit is proposed or anticipated?
  • Possible benefits may include:
    • Environmental repair.
    • Open space.
    • Waterfront access.
    • Housing.
    • Jobs.
    • Community facilities.
    • Mobility.
    • Public health.
    • Ecological restoration.
    • Neighborhood connectivity.

⚠️ Tradeoff or Unresolved Question

  • What challenge, concern, tradeoff, or unanswered question remains?
  • Possible concerns may include:
    • Cost.
    • Contamination.
    • Access.
    • Ownership.
    • Long-term maintenance.
    • Infrastructure limitations.
    • Displacement.
    • Equity.
    • Public benefit versus private development.

🖼️ Part 2: Create a Brownfield Transformation Profile

Your group will create one concise presentation artifact: a Brownfield Transformation Profile.

The profile may be a single slide, one-page PDF, or compact presentation page. It should be visually clear and use planning-document evidence.

Required Components

Your Brownfield Transformation Profile must include:

  1. Planning Area Identification
    • Planning-area name.
    • Borough and neighborhood.
    • EPIC planning-area link.
  2. Existing Condition
    • A short description of what exists now.
    • One planning-document image, map, diagram, photograph, or excerpt.
  3. Issue or Constraint
    • One environmental, industrial, infrastructural, land-use, or spatial issue that matters.
  4. Future Transformation
    • A concise description of the proposed or anticipated reuse, remediation, redevelopment, or planning strategy.
  5. Public Benefit
    • One potential public, environmental, mobility, housing, ecological, or community benefit.
  6. Tradeoff or Question
    • One concern, tradeoff, limitation, or unresolved question.
  7. Source Citation
    • Cite the EPIC planning-area page and any planning PDF, image, map, or document used.

🗣️ Part 3: Group Presentation

Each group will present its Brownfield Transformation Profile during the final hour of class.

Your presentation should be concise and address:

  1. Where is the planning area?
  2. What is the existing condition?
  3. What industrial, infrastructural, environmental, or remediation issue matters?
  4. What future transformation is proposed or being considered?
  5. What is one potential public benefit?
  6. What is one concern, tradeoff, or unresolved question?

Comparative Discussion

As groups present, we will compare:

  • How different planning areas define reuse, remediation, redevelopment, or replacement.
  • What existing assets are being retained, repaired, transformed, or removed.
  • What kinds of public benefit are most frequently proposed.
  • What constraints appear most difficult.
  • How planning documents represent existing conditions and future possibilities.
  • How these questions might apply to the High Line and Hudson Yards.

📚 Homework Reading for Class 9

For Class 9, read:

Ilaria Curulli, Reuse or Abuse? Ethics in Requalification Design

Read:

Section 1: “Wasted and Reclaimed Landscapes”

Access the reading

This section examines how industrial, infrastructural, and post-industrial landscapes are often treated as obsolete or disposable. Curulli asks how these sites can be understood as existing urban resources rather than blank land awaiting replacement.

As you read, consider:

  • What makes a landscape appear “wasted” or obsolete?
  • What physical, historical, environmental, or social qualities might remain valuable?
  • What is the difference between reclaiming a site and simply replacing it?
  • How should designers and planners study an existing condition before proposing a new use?
  • How might these questions apply to the EPIC Place-Based Community Brownfield Planning Areas?
  • How might this framework help us interpret the High Line and Hudson Yards?
