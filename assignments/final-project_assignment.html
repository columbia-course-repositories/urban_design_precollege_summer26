Urban Planning and Design: Final Project Design + Deliverable Assignment

Columbia University | Pre-College Programs | Urban Planning and Design | Summer 2026

Published

July 12, 2026

🏙️ Project Overview

Working in teams of 3–4 students, you will investigate one assigned observation territory within the High Line and Hudson Yards district.

Although every group studies the same overall project, each observation territory presents different spatial conditions, public spaces, and urban design questions. Your territory will become the foundation of your investigation.

The purpose of this project is not simply to document the site, but to understand how it works. Through observation, mapping, drawing, modeling, and critical analysis, your group will develop an evidence-based critique of your portion of the High Line and Hudson Yards.

The final product will be a professional presentation assembled in Adobe InDesign and presented to the class during the final studio review.

🎯 Learning Objectives

By the completion of this project you should be able to:

  • Conduct structured urban field observations.
  • Document cities through sketches, photography, and written notes.
  • Develop analytical diagrams from direct observations.
  • Use GIS to understand urban context.
  • Construct analytical physical models.
  • Apply urban design theory to real places.
  • Develop an evidence-based urban design critique.
  • Communicate complex ideas through a professional presentation.

📍 Observation Territories

Each group will be assigned one observation territory within the High Line and Hudson Yards district.

Although every group investigates the same overall project, each territory presents different spatial conditions and opportunities for analysis.

Your assigned territory will serve as the starting point for your investigation. While every group will eventually experience the entire site, your observations should remain grounded in your assigned territory.

(Observation territories have already been assigned by the instructor.)

🔄 Project Workflow

1. Observe

Collect evidence in the field.

  • Observation notes
  • Urban sketches
  • Annotated photography
  • Movement studies
  • Sightline observations
  • Public life observations

2. Analyze

Interpret your observations.

Develop:

  • Spatial diagrams
  • Movement diagrams
  • GIS context maps
  • Building/open space relationships
  • Behavioral observations
  • Spatial organization

3. Represent

Communicate your findings through multiple forms of representation.

Produce:

  • Adobe InDesign presentation
  • Urban sketches
  • GIS maps
  • Analytical diagrams
  • Annotated photographs
  • Physical site model

4. Critique

Develop an evidence-based argument.

Ask:

  • How does this space function?
  • What works well?
  • What does not work?
  • Why?
  • What evidence supports these conclusions?

🗺️ GIS Context Mapping

Each group will create a distance study buffer around its assigned observation territory.

Tip

Tip: work with instructor and TA to customize the buffer distance to be fit the territory analyses objectives.

Using QGIS, develop a series of context maps that place your territory within the larger urban environment.

Suggested mapping topics include:

  • Building footprints
  • Land use
  • Transit infrastructure
  • Parks and open space
  • Street network
  • Historical imagery
  • Environmental conditions
  • Additional datasets explored during the course

Each map should include:

  • Observation Territory
  • Distance Study Area Buffer
  • High Line
  • Hudson Yards Project Area
  • North Arrow
  • Scale Bar
  • Map Title
  • Data Source

These maps should provide spatial context and support your field observations.

Demographic Context

Students may also incorporate screen captures from Morphocode to illustrate demographic and urban characteristics surrounding their observation territory.

Possible themes include:

  • Population density
  • Employment density
  • Residential population
  • Household income
  • Housing
  • Commuting patterns
  • Additional demographic indicators relevant to the project

Morphocode graphics should be used as supporting evidence rather than the primary focus of the analysis.

Students should always explain how demographic conditions relate to their field observations and urban design critique.

📚 Analytical Framework

Your critique should draw upon the ideas and methods explored throughout the course.

Adaptive Reuse

Consider how existing infrastructure has been transformed into new public space and development.

How successful is this transformation?

Kevin Lynch

Use Lynch’s vocabulary to describe the spatial organization of your territory.

Examples include:

  • Paths
  • Edges
  • Districts
  • Nodes
  • Landmarks

Jan Gehl

Evaluate the public life of your territory.

Consider:

  • Human scale
  • Pedestrian movement
  • Places to stay
  • Places to move
  • Public life
  • Comfort
  • Activity

Jane Jacobs vs. Robert Moses

Evaluate Hudson Yards and the High Line as examples of urban renewal.

Questions might include:

  • Does the project support vibrant public life?
  • Does it encourage diversity of use?
  • How public does the space actually feel?
  • Who appears to benefit from the redevelopment?
  • What role does large-scale planning play in the project?

Reuse Ethics

Draw upon the assigned reading to evaluate adaptive reuse as both a design strategy and an ethical position.

👥 Team Organization

Each team consists of 3–4 students. While every student contributes to field observations, analysis, discussion, and the final presentation, each member should assume primary responsibility for one aspect of the project.

Observation Lead

Coordinates field observations and documentation.

Primary responsibilities include:

  • Organizing field notes
  • Recording observations
  • Conducting movement studies
  • Conducting behavior studies
  • Coordinating evidence collection

Representation Lead

Develops visual representations of the site.

Primary responsibilities include:

  • Urban sketches
  • Analytical diagrams
  • Sightline studies
  • Annotating drawings
  • Annotating photographs

GIS & Mapping Lead

Develops spatial context and mapping.

Primary responsibilities include:

  • QGIS mapping
  • 0.5-mile study buffer
  • Context maps
  • Morphocode demographic graphics
  • Data organization
  • Map production

Modeling Lead

Develops the physical analytical model of the observation territory.

Primary responsibilities include:

  • Physical site model
  • Site massing studies
  • Model construction
  • Model refinement
  • Model photography for presentation

Presentation Lead

Coordinates the digital presentation and overall visual communication.

Primary responsibilities include:

  • Adobe InDesign layout
  • Graphic organization
  • Page composition
  • Figure captions and labeling
  • Presentation assembly

  • Presentation coordination

Shared Responsibilities

All team members are expected to participate in:

  • Field observations
  • Discussion and analysis
  • Critical evaluation
  • Presentation preparation
  • Final presentation

Every student should be prepared to discuss both their individual contribution and the overall findings of the group.

💬 Final Considerations

The purpose of this project is not simply to describe a place.

Your goal is to combine observation, mapping, drawing, modeling, research, and urban design theory into a thoughtful and evidence-based argument about how your portion of the High Line and Hudson Yards functions as an urban environment.

Support every conclusion with evidence.

The strongest projects will move beyond description to demonstrate a clear understanding of how urban design influences the experience of the city, recognizing both the successes and limitations of one of New York City’s most significant contemporary redevelopment projects.
