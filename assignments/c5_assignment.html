📋 Assignment Overview

In Class 4, we used historical maps and imagery to investigate how cities change over time. In this assignment, we shift our attention to a different question:

What is the city made of?

Cities are composed of physical elements that shape movement, development, navigation, and everyday experience. Streets, blocks, parcels, buildings, infrastructure, and open spaces combine to create urban form—the physical structure of the city.

In this assignment, you will use GIS and field observation to investigate urban form around your observation point and the Columbia University study area.

GIS is one of several tools we will use to investigate urban form. The goal is not to learn advanced GIS techniques, but rather to better understand how the physical structure of cities influences how people experience, understand, and navigate urban environments.

Note

Key Question What physical structures help people understand, navigate, and experience a city?

🎯 Learning Objectives

By completing this assignment, students will be able to:

  • Identify major elements of urban form.
  • Interpret urban form using maps, GIS, and field observation.
  • Apply concepts from Kevin Lynch’s The Image of the City.
  • Connect mapped information to lived urban experience.
  • Compare the city as represented in GIS with the city experienced on the ground.

🗺️ Exercise 1: Urban Form Investigation in GIS

Introduction

Urban form can be studied through a variety of spatial datasets. Today we will use GIS to investigate the physical structure of the city surrounding our observation areas.

We will utiize vector datasets readily available from NYC Planning Resources:

Available Datasets

  • LION
  • Tax Lots / Parcels
  • MapPLUTO
  • Zoning
  • Building Footprints
  • Open Space and Parks

Tasks

Using GIS, revisit your observation point and investigate the surrounding area.

Identify and Examine

  • Streets
  • Blocks
  • Parcels
  • Buildings
  • Infrastructure
  • Open Space

Consider

  • How is movement organized?
  • What appears to structure the area?

📍 Transition Exercise: Reading Urban Form

Introduction

Cities can be understood from both the street and the map. Kevin Lynch argued that people often use recurring elements to understand and navigate urban environments. In this exercise, you will begin identifying these elements using GIS and class discussion.

Tasks

Using GIS and class discussion, identify examples of:

  • Path
  • Edge
  • District
  • Node
  • Landmark

near your observation point or within the Columbia study area.

Consider

  • Which elements are easiest to identify?
  • Which are most difficult?
  • What becomes visible on a map that is difficult to see from the street?
  • What becomes visible on the street that is difficult to see on a map?

📍 Exercise 2: Field Investigation

Introduction

Maps and GIS provide one way of understanding cities. Field observation provides another.

In this exercise, you will compare mapped urban form with experienced urban form by revisiting your observation area and documenting how the physical structure of the city influences movement, navigation, and everyday experience.

Tasks

Visit your observation point or nearby study area.

Document:

  • Streets and circulation patterns
  • Building types and scale
  • Open spaces
  • Infrastructure
  • Areas of activity
  • Areas of transition
  • Notable landmarks

Use:

  • Notes
  • Sketches
  • Photographs

to record your observations.

Consider

  • Does the city feel the same as it appears in GIS?
  • What features seem most important when walking through the area?
  • What physical elements help people understand where they are?
  • What physical elements influence movement?

🏙️ Exercise 3: Urban Form Analysis

Introduction

The goal of this assignment is not simply to create a map. The goal is to use maps, GIS, and field observation together to understand the physical structure of a city.

In this final section, you will synthesize your GIS investigation and field observations into a map and written reflection.

Create a Map

Using QGIS, create a PDF map showing:

  • Observation Point
  • Streets
  • Parcels
  • Buildings
  • Additional urban form elements of your choice

Your map should clearly communicate the physical structure of the study area.

✍️ Reflection

Write a short response (approximately 250–400 words) addressing the following questions:

  1. Which elements of urban form seem most important in your study area?

  2. Which of Lynch’s five elements (path, edge, district, node, or landmark) is most visible?

  3. How did GIS help you understand the physical structure of this place?

  4. What aspects of urban form were easier to understand in the field than on the map?
