Jane Jacobs Reading Discussion (25–30 Minutes)

🎯 Discussion Goal

Today’s discussion will function as a design jury. Students will examine competing ideas about how cities should be planned while considering both large-scale infrastructure and neighborhood-scale urban life.

11:30 – 11:50 am

Opening Question

Project the following question:

Who builds better cities?

  • Engineers and planners who design large urban systems?
  • Local residents who understand neighborhoods?

Students should not answer immediately. Choose an answer privately and keep it in mind throughout the discussion.

11:25 – 11:30 am

Pair Challenge

Students turn to a nearby classmate.

One student is assigned to defend Robert Moses.

The other student is assigned to defend Jane Jacobs.

Students should defend their assigned position regardless of their own opinion.

Robert Moses

Complete the following statements:

  • Cities need __________.
  • Large infrastructure is necessary because __________.
  • Neighborhood opposition sometimes prevents __________.

Jane Jacobs

Complete the following statements:

  • Cities succeed because __________.
  • Streets become safe when __________.
  • Large plans fail because __________.

Spend several minutes discussing and preparing arguments.

11:30 – 11:40 am

Design Jury

Several selected pairs will present brief arguments.

  • One pair presents the Robert Moses position.
  • A second pair responds from Jane Jacobs’ perspective.
  • The class votes informally on which argument was more persuasive before moving to the next exchange.

11:40 – 11:50 am

Whole-Class Discussion

Organized Complexity

  • Jacobs describes cities as problems of organized complexity.
  • What does she mean?
  • Is a city more like a machine or an ecosystem?

Order versus Urban Life

  • Can a city be highly organized and still fail as a place for people?
  • What is the difference between order and vitality?

Diversity

  • Jacobs argues that diversity creates successful cities.
  • What kinds of diversity does she mean?
    • Land uses?
    • Buildings?
    • People?
    • Activities?
    • Times of day?

Engineering versus Community

  • Why might Jacobs frustrate engineers?
  • Why might engineers frustrate Jacobs?
  • Can both perspectives be valuable?

Decision Making

Imagine Columbia University plans to redesign the IAB and its plaza area that we visited yesterday.

Who should have the greatest influence?

  • Engineers
  • Urban designers
  • Local residents
  • Businesses
  • City government

Explain your reasoning.

Is Jacobs Always Right?

  • Are there situations where large-scale planning is necessary?
  • How might Jacobs respond to:
    • Climate change?
    • Transit systems?
    • Regional infrastructure?
    • Flood protection?

Closing Reflection

Return to the opening question.

Who builds better cities?

  • Engineers and planners?
  • Communities?
  • Both working together?

Ask for a show of hands.

Concluding Quesiton:

Did anyone change their mind during today’s discussion? Why?
