Class Time | Location

Important

Important: Class 4 builds directly from the Class 3 mapping exercise. Students should bring their Assignment 3 observation map PDF, Class 2 sketch, recorded coordinates, sketchbook, notes, and laptop to both sessions.

Class 4 Agenda Items - AM Session

11:10 - 11:20 am

  • Attendance and informal announcements.
  • Brief review of the day’s focus: Cities Through Time: Historical Growth and Urban Transformation.

11:20 - 11:50 am

  • Assignment 3 Sharing: Observation Maps
    • Students share exported observation map PDFs from Class 3.
    • Discussion of how each observation point changed when represented on a map.
    • Reflection on the relationship between observation, location, and geographic context.
    • Comparison of street-level observation and map-based representation.

11:50 am - 12:10 pm

  • Reading Discussion:
    • James Corner: The Agency of Mapping.
    • Maps as selective and interpretive representations.
    • Mapping as a tool for discovery, analysis, and design.
    • Discussion of how maps influence what we notice, understand, and remember about cities.
    • Reflection on how mapping can reveal hidden spatial relationships and historical processes.

12:10 - 12:35 pm

  • Class 4 Lecture: Cities Through Time
    • How do cities change over time?
    • Historical maps as evidence of urban transformation.
    • Historical aerial imagery and visible urban change.
    • HOLC redlining maps as historical spatial documents.
    • David Rumsey historical maps and urban form.
    • Comparing past and present urban conditions.
    • How historical mapping prepares us to read and remember cities.

12:35 - 1:00 pm

  • Transition Workshop: Historical Maps and GIS
    • Revisiting observation points from Class 3.
    • Understanding historical maps as spatial evidence.
    • Bringing historical maps into a GIS workflow.
    • Comparing contemporary basemaps with historical representations.
    • Preparing for the afternoon historical imagery and urban change investigation.

Class 4 Agenda Items - PM Session

3:10 - 3:40 pm

  • Assignment 4 Introduction - Class 4 Assignment
    • Review of assignment goals and deliverables.
    • Introduction to historical comparison as an urban design method.
    • Review of required resources:
      • HOLC / Mapping Inequality.
      • NYC Then & Now historical aerial imagery.
      • David Rumsey historical maps.
    • Discussion of how historical evidence can be compared with present-day observations.
    • Questions and setup support.

3:40 - 4:30 pm

  • Historical Imagery and Urban Change Workshop - Class 4 Assignment
    • Students revisit their observation point or nearby study area.
    • Students examine historical aerial imagery using NYC Then & Now.
    • Students examine HOLC maps and area descriptions through Mapping Inequality.
    • Students review selected David Rumsey historical maps.
    • Students identify evidence of urban change, including:
      • Street patterns.
      • Building patterns.
      • Open space.
      • Infrastructure.
      • Land use.
      • Neighborhood boundaries.
      • Urban redevelopment.
    • Students compare what is visible in historical maps and aerial imagery with what they observed in the field.

4:30 - 4:50 pm

Historical Mapping Discussion and Troubleshooting

Important

Important: Students should save screenshots, notes, links, and exported materials before the end of class. These materials will support the Class 4 assignment submission and Friday’s discussion of how cities are read and remembered.

  • Students review historical imagery and mapping findings.
  • Discussion of what changed and what remained.
  • Troubleshooting access to HOLC, NYC Then & Now, and David Rumsey resources.
  • Discussion of how historical maps represent cities differently from contemporary GIS basemaps.
  • Discussion of how historical evidence can reshape our understanding of a familiar place.

4:50 - 5:00 pm

  • Reflection and Wrap-Up
    • What changed in your study area over time?
    • What remained consistent?
    • What did historical imagery reveal that contemporary maps did not?
    • How do historical maps shape how we understand urban memory and transformation?

Week 1 Class 4 Task List

  1. Attend Week 1 | Class 4 Meeting (07/02/26) starting at 11:10 a.m.–1:00 p.m. and then resuming 3:10–5:00 p.m.
  2. Participate in the Assignment 3 sharing discussion and Corner reading discussion.
  3. Review posted materials; ask instructor any remaining questions or areas of clarification.
  4. Conduct Class 4 Assignment across both sessions.
  5. Complete the day’s homework assignment located at the end of Class 4 Assignment.
