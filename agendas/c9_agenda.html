Important

Important: Class 9 builds directly from the Class 8 discussion of adaptive reuse, brownfield planning, industrial land, environmental repair, and urban transformation. Students should bring their sketchbook, notes, and be prepared to document observations during the High Line and Hudson Yards field trip. Students will travel with the instructor and teaching assistant to and from the field trip site.

Class 9 Agenda Items - AM Session

11:10 - 11:15 am

  • Attendance and announcements.

  • Brief review of the day’s focus: Adaptive Reuse, Public Space, and Urban Transformation

  • Reading Discussion: Ilaria Curulli, Reuse or Abuse? Ethics in Requalification Design

    • Focus: Section 1, “Wasted and Reclaimed Landscapes.”
    • Discussion of industrial, infrastructural, and post-industrial landscapes.
    • Reuse, reclamation, redevelopment, and replacement.
    • Existing urban conditions as resources rather than blank sites.
    • Ethical questions in adaptive reuse and urban transformation.

Discussion Questions

  • What makes a landscape appear “wasted” or obsolete?
  • Why should former industrial or infrastructural sites not be treated as blank land?
  • What physical, historical, environmental, or social qualities might remain valuable?
  • What is the difference between reclaiming a site and replacing it?
  • What should designers and planners study before proposing a new use?
  • How does this reading help us think about the High Line and Hudson Yards?

    • Observation expectations.
    • Field documentation requirements.

Observation Territories

Each group is responsible for one primary observation territory. Begin your investigation here, documenting the site through sketches, photographs, notes, and diagrams. While all groups will eventually experience the entire High Line and Hudson Yards district, your assigned territory should serve as the foundation for your analysis and final presentation.

Group A — The High Line: Chelsea Approach

Study Area

  • High Line from Gansevoort Street to approximately West 27th Street

Primary Focus

  • Adaptive reuse
  • Landscape design
  • Human scale
  • Movement along the linear park
  • Changing views of the city

Group B — The High Line Transition

Study Area

  • High Line from West 28th Street to Hudson Yards (approximately West 34th Street)

Primary Focus

  • Transition from historic infrastructure to new development
  • The Spur
  • Rail yard overlook
  • Changing spatial character
  • Arrival sequence into Hudson Yards

Group C — Hudson Yards Core

Study Area

  • Public Square and Gardens
  • The Shed
  • Vessel
  • Retail podium
  • Adjacent tower entrances

Primary Focus

  • Public space
  • Building massing
  • Pedestrian activity
  • Gathering spaces
  • Relationship between buildings and open space

Group D — Hudson Boulevard & West Edge

Study Area

  • Hudson Boulevard
  • Bella Abzug Park
  • Western edge of Hudson Yards
  • Javits Center interface
  • Hudson River connection

Primary Focus

  • District-scale planning
  • Open space network
  • Connections to the surrounding city
  • Landscape design
  • Relationship between Hudson Yards and the waterfront

Class 9 Agenda Items - PM Session

  • Travel to the High Line field trip site.

High Line and Hudson Yards

  • High Line
  • Hudson Yards
  • Surrounding redevelopment district

Observation Questions

  • What parts of the former rail infrastructure remain visible?
  • How does the High Line transform infrastructure into public space?
  • What new uses, programs, and experiences have been introduced?
  • How does the project relate to surrounding buildings and streets?
  • Where is the industrial past preserved, revealed, or erased?
  • How does Hudson Yards change the meaning and context of the High Line?
  • Who appears to benefit from this transformation?
  • What tradeoffs or tensions are visible in the built environment?
