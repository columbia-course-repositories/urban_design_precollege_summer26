✏️ Exercise: Drawing the City from Above

Introduction

Cities can be experienced from many viewpoints. Most of our daily experience occurs at street level, but planners and urban designers often begin by looking from above.

A bird’s-eye view reveals relationships that are difficult to recognize while walking:

  • Street networks
  • Blocks
  • Districts
  • Open space
  • Infrastructure
  • Patterns of development

This exercise is not about creating an accurate map. It is about identifying the physical structure that makes a city recognizable.

Drawing Exercise (15 Minutes)

Choose the city, town, or neighborhood you know best.

From memory, create a simple bird’s-eye view sketch showing the physical structure of the place.

Do not worry about precise scale or perfect geometry.

Instead, focus on the elements that organize the city.

Include, where appropriate:

  • Major streets or circulation routes
  • Rivers, coastlines, or other edges
  • Neighborhoods or districts
  • Parks and open spaces
  • Important landmarks
  • Transit or infrastructure
  • Areas of activity or gathering
Guiding Principles

As you draw, ask yourself:

  • What physical features make this city immediately recognizable?
  • Which streets organize movement?
  • What boundaries separate one area from another?
  • Where do people gather?
  • Which landmarks help people orient themselves?
  • What would someone need to see to recognize this place instantly?

Remember:

Draw the structure of the city—not every detail.

Reflection

After completing your drawing, compare it with classmates.

Consider:

  • Which features appeared in nearly every drawing?
  • Which elements were unique to individual memories?
  • What was easiest to remember?
  • What was difficult to represent?
  • How did viewing the city from above change your understanding of it?
