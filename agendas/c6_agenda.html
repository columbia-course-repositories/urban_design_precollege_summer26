Class Time | Location

  • ⏰ Time: 11:10 a.m.–1:00 p.m. and 1:00–5:00 p.m.

Important

Important: Class 6 builds directly from the Class 5 Urban Form Investigation and Kevin Lynch reading. Students should bring their sketchbook, notes, laptop, and any materials from their urban form investigation. Students should also be prepared to take notes and document observations during the Museum of the City of New York field trip.

Class 6 Agenda Items - AM Session

11:10 - 11:20 am

  • Attendance and announcements.
  • Brief review of the day’s focus: Reading and Representing Cities: Bird’s-Eye Views, Models, and Urban Memory

11:20 - 11:50 am

  • Class 5 Sharing: Urban Form Investigation
    • Review of GIS and field investigation findings.
    • Discussion of streets, parcels, buildings, infrastructure, and open space.
    • Student examples of paths, edges, districts, nodes, and landmarks.
    • Comparing mapped urban form and experienced urban form.

11:50 am - 12:15 pm

  • Reading Discussion:
    • Kevin Lynch: The Image of the City
    • Paths, edges, districts, nodes, and landmarks.
    • Mental maps and urban memory.
    • What makes a city memorable?
    • What makes a city easy to navigate?
    • Which Lynch element seems most important in New York City?
    • How do physical structures shape the image of a city?

12:15 - 12:50 pm

  • Class 6 Lecture
    • Reading and Representing Cities
    • Bird’s-Eye Views and Urban Understanding
    • Aerial Imagery and Urban Observation
    • Physical Models and Urban Form
    • Joe Macken’s New York City Model
    • Representation as a Tool for Understanding Cities
    • Preparing to Read Cities from Above

12:50 - 1:00 pm

  • Field Trip Orientation
    • Museum schedule and logistics.
    • Overview of exhibits.
    • Observation expectations.
    • Reflection exercise introduction.
    • Guiding questions for the visit.

Class 6 Agenda Items - PM Session

1:00 - 1:30 pm

  • Travel to the Museum of the City of New York

1:30 - 4:00 pm

Field Trip 1

Museum of the City of New York

  • He Built This City: Joe Macken’s Model
  • New York at Its Core

Observation Questions

  • How does a model represent a city differently than a map?
  • What becomes visible from above?
  • What aspects of urban form become easier to understand?
  • How does scale influence urban representation?
  • What information is emphasized?
  • What information is omitted?

4:00 - 4:45 pm

  • Return to Columbia University

4:45 - 5:00 pm

Reflection and Wrap-Up

  • Initial observations from the museum visit.
  • Discussion of bird’s-eye urban representation.
  • Comparing maps, aerial imagery, and physical models.
  • Introduction to the museum reflection exercise.

Looking Ahead

Reading (Discussion - AM Session, Class 8)

  • Reading #1:
    • What do you need to learn to pass the Knowledge?
  • Reading #2:
    • Cache Cab: Taxi Drivers’ Brains Grow to Navigate London’s Streets

Guiding Question

How do people learn and remember the structure of a city without relying on maps or GPS?

As You Read, Consider

  • What is The Knowledge?
  • Why does it take years to learn?
  • How do taxi drivers build mental maps of London?
  • How is learning a city different from following GPS directions?
  • What connections can you make between The Knowledge and Kevin Lynch’s ideas about paths, edges, districts, nodes, and landmarks?

  3. Attend the Class 6 lecture and field trip orientation.
  4. Complete field observations and notes during the Museum of the City of New York visit.
  5. Complete the museum reflection exercise and assigned reading before Class 7.
