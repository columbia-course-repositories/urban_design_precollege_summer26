Class Time | Location

  • Location: 140 URIS HALL / East Side Coastal Resiliency Project Site
Important

Important: Class 10 begins Week 3 and shifts from adaptive reuse and urban transformation toward infrastructure, resilience, environmental risk, and final project thinking. Students should bring their sketchbook, notes, laptop, and be prepared to document observations during the East Side Coastal Resiliency field trip. Students will travel with the instructor and teaching assistant to and from the field trip site.

Class 10 Agenda Items - AM Session

11:10 - 11:20 am

  • Attendance and announcements.
  • Brief review of the day’s focus: Infrastructure, Resilience, and Urban Systems

11:20 - 11:35 am

  • Brief Field Trip 2 Review: High Line and Hudson Yards
    • Initial reflections from Friday’s field trip.
    • Adaptive reuse, public space, and redevelopment.
    • Reuse as a catalyst for district-scale transformation.
    • Visible benefits, tradeoffs, and tensions.
    • Transition from reuse and redevelopment to infrastructure and resilience.

11:35 am - 12:20 pm

  • Class 10 Lecture
    • Infrastructure and urban systems.
    • Climate risk and coastal cities.
    • Flooding, storm surge, and sea-level rise.
    • Resilience as an urban design and planning challenge.
    • Public space, infrastructure, and environmental protection.
    • East Side Coastal Resiliency Project.
    • Designing for risk, access, ecology, and everyday urban life.
    • Preparing to read resilience infrastructure in the field.

12:20 - 12:50 pm

  • Site Tour Question Workshop
    • Students work with the instructor to develop questions for the East Side Coastal Resiliency site visit.
    • Questions should address:
      • Project goals.
      • Climate risk and flood protection.
      • Public space design.
      • Community impacts.
      • Construction and phasing.
      • Ecology and landscape systems.
      • Access and equity.
      • Long-term maintenance.
      • The role of design professionals in large-scale urban infrastructure projects.

12:50 - 1:00 pm

  • Field Trip Orientation
    • Travel logistics.
    • Site route and meeting points.
    • Guest speaker introduction:
      • Molly Bourne, Principal Planner, MNLA.
    • Observation expectations.
    • Field documentation requirements.
    • Guiding questions for the site tour.

Class 10 Agenda Items - PM Session

1:00 - 1:30 pm

  • Travel to the East Side Coastal Resiliency Project site.

1:30 - 4:00 pm

Field Trip 3

East Side Coastal Resiliency Project

  • Site tour with Molly Bourne, Principal Planner, MNLA
  • Observation of resilience infrastructure, public space, landscape systems, and urban context.

Observation Questions

  • What environmental risks is the project responding to?
  • How does the project combine infrastructure and public space?
  • What parts of the design are protective?
  • What parts of the design support everyday public use?
  • How does the project change access to the waterfront?
  • How does construction, phasing, or disruption affect surrounding communities?
  • What ecological or landscape strategies are visible?
  • What design decisions seem most important at the site scale?
  • What questions remain about equity, maintenance, and long-term resilience?

4:00 - 4:45 pm

  • Return to Columbia University.

4:45 - 5:00 pm

Reflection and Wrap-Up

  • Initial observations from the East Side Coastal Resiliency site visit.
  • Discussion of infrastructure, resilience, and public space.
  • Connecting the field trip to final project thinking.
  • Preview of Tuesday’s transition to final project introduction and site selection.

Looking Ahead

Tuesday 07/14

  • Final project introduction.
  • Group formation.
  • Site selection.
  • Project framing.
  • Spatial analysis and documentation.

  4. Help develop site-tour questions for the East Side Coastal Resiliency visit.

  6. Prepare for Tuesday’s final project introduction and site selection.
